

This is headed toward a short expository note—more paper/blog post than lecture notes. The referee comments are strong, but §§7–8 need one further precision.

Take these edits:

Remove the unnecessary \(\cap{|g|\le k}\).

State that \(\pi\) is contractive, so \(\ker\pi\) is a closed self-adjoint ideal and the quotient is a \(C^*\)-algebra.

Replace “injective \(\Rightarrow\) faithful” with “injective/faithful.”

Add the concrete norm identity


\[
  \|M_f\|
  =
  \|[f]\|_{B/\ker\pi}
  =
  \inf_{h\in\ker\pi}\|f+h\|_\infty
  =
  \|f\|_{\infty,\mathrm{loc}},
\]

\[
  \|f\|_{\infty,\mathrm{loc}}
  =
  \inf\{c\ge0:\{|f|>c\}\text{ is locally null}\}.
\]

The infinite singleton example is correct, but it makes \(L^2={0}\), introducing a distracting convention about whether the zero algebra is unital. A cleaner example is

\[
X=[0,1]\sqcup\{p\},
\]

\[
L^2(X,\mu)\cong L^2[0,1],
\]

\[
M_{\chi_{\{p\}}}=0.
\]

The deeper correction is this: after quotienting by locally null functions, the relevant general condition is not necessarily localizability of the original nonsemifinite measure. Define its semifinite reduction

\[
\mu_{\mathrm{sf}}(E)
=
\sup\{\mu(F):F\subseteq E,\ F\in\Sigma,\ \mu(F)<\infty\}.
\]

\[
\mathcal N_{\mu_{\mathrm{sf}}}
=
\mathcal N_{\mathrm{loc}},
\qquad
B(X,\Sigma)/\ker\pi
\cong
L^\infty(\mu_{\mathrm{sf}}).
\]

Consequently the precise structural theorem is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{\mathrm{sf}}\text{ is localizable}
&\iff
\Sigma/\mathcal N_{\mathrm{loc}}
\text{ is Dedekind complete}\\
&\iff
B/\ker\pi\longrightarrow (L^1(\mu))^*
\text{ is onto}\\
&\iff
\overline\pi(B/\ker\pi)
\text{ is a von Neumann algebra}\\
&\iff
\overline\pi(B/\ker\pi)
\text{ is a masa in }B(L^2(\mu)).
\end{aligned}
\]

Cohn is more than a general literature anchor: he explicitly constructs \(L^\infty\) using bounded measurable functions modulo local-a.e. equality and proves the canonical map into \((L^1)^*\) is always isometric. Cohn, §§3.3 and 3.5. The arbitrary-measure condition above is treated explicitly as semilocalizability by De Pauw. For the modern operator-algebra equivalences, Blecher–Goldstein–Labuschagne, Theorem 5.2 is the clean citation. Under Fremlin’s convention, “localizable” already includes semifiniteness, so “semifinite and localizable” is redundant.

With that adjustment, §§7–8 become the real climax: the representation theorem and the \(L^1\)-duality theorem are literally two manifestations of the same quotient.
